# References: what makes a view feel like awe

Notes from research on 2026-11-18, kept so later changes to the view have something to be judged against. Links only; no reference images are copied here.

## What the research says about awe

- Awe has two parts: **vastness** (something far bigger than you) and **accommodation** (your mind has to stretch to take it in). Keltner and Haidt, 2003, as summarized in [The Awe Experience](https://www.beautifulminds-newsletter.com/p/the-awe-experience).
- Nature is tied to awe more than to any other good feeling (Anderson, Monroy and Keltner, 2018), as summarized in [The Psychology of Awe in Nature](https://psychologytoday.com/sg/blog/finding-new-home/201807/the-psychology-awe-awe-in-nature).
- Awe makes people feel small, in three cultures. [Nature research summary on awe](https://www.nature.com/research-intelligence/nri-topic-summaries/the-psychology-and-effects-of-awe-micro-160502).
- The pictures used in studies to cause awe are wide views: from a mountain top, or inside a canyon. [MDPI, Sustainability 13(14)](https://mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/14/8025).
- No study was found that ranks kinds of scene (sunset, mountains, night sky) by awe. One preprint says such ratings do not exist yet. [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10370647/v1).
- Painters of the sublime (Bierstadt and others) used very large scale, strong contrast between bright light and deep shade, and weather. [Landscape Visions: Landscapes of awe](https://eclecticlight.co/2015/07/20/landscape-visions-3-landscapes-of-awe/).

## What the Firewatch look is made of

From write-ups of the artists' own talks:

- It started from one painting by Olly Moss; Jane Ng built it in 3D. Flat simple shapes, strong silhouettes, little detail inside them. [GDC 2015: The Art of Firewatch](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch)
- Layered colors give the forest horizon that looks endless. [Further looking into the art of Firewatch](https://blogs.ulster.ac.uk/bethbuchanan/2021/03/05/ixd104-further-looking-into-the-art-of-firewatch)
- Colored fog does most of the depth: each layer fades toward the fog color. [How a small art team created Firewatch's environment](https://boingboing.net/2015/03/27/how-a-small-art-team-created-f.html)
- Far trees get less detailed and more stylized with distance. [Jane Ng on Firewatch's wilderness](https://www.pcgamesn.com/firewatch/there-was-a-lot-of-copy-pasting-jane-ng-talks-creating-firewatchs-wyoming-wilderness)

## What I saw in the reference pictures

Looked at image search results for the game's key art, for photos of layered ridges at sunrise, and for flat sunset illustrations.

- One color ramp for the whole picture: pale sky, then each nearer layer one step darker and redder, ending in a near-black foreground. No second color family.
- The sky is almost flat. No cartoon clouds, at most thin streaks.
- Forest is drawn as the ridge's edge: many conifer tips of uneven height in the ridge's own flat color.
- Each layer is lighter at its foot, where the fog sits in the valley.
- One bright focal point: a low sun in a gap between peaks, or a river that reflects the sky.
- The darkest layer is the nearest one, with tall trees at the sides that frame the view.

## In-game views and real photographs

Looked at a second time, because the first pass leaned on the game's poster art, and the views inside the game and real places are what set the bar.

In-game sunset views:

- Everything is bathed in one warm light. The sun is a soft bright patch with a wide bloom, not a hard disc.
- Rows of conifers stand one behind another, each row lighter and more golden the closer it is to the sun.
- Haze glows around the sun and washes out whatever is near it. Light shafts show in that haze.
- Water picks up the gold of the sky.

Real photographs (Grand Teton from the Snake River, Yosemite's Tunnel View, layered ridges of the Smoky Mountains at sunrise):

- A river in an S-curve leads the eye to the peaks (the picture Ansel Adams made famous at the Snake River Overlook).
- Fog lies in the valleys, so each ridge stands clear of the next.
- Dark conifers in front frame a bright far distance.
- Clouds are lit from below in orange and pink while their tops stay dusky.
- Peaks are tall and sharp against the sky, far higher than anything near.

## Rules this theme follows

1. One warm ramp: pale gold sky to dark plum foreground, eight steps.
2. Many layers (vastness), each fading to a lighter foot (fog).
3. Forest as ragged silhouettes on the ridge edge, smaller and simpler with distance.
4. A low sun in a notch between the far peaks, with a bloom that washes the nearby layers toward gold, and a river that carries its light into the valley.
5. Tall dark firs at both sides, so you look out from shelter.
6. Tiny birds against huge mountains, to give the scale.
7. Slow motion only: nothing that pulls the eye away from reading.
